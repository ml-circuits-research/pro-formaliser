Design decisions and unresolved issues

Descrierea țintei și direcția mutării

În „push the woman in green back”, construcția „in green” selectează femeia, iar „back” stabilește direcția. Lumea declară inventarul de substantive, inventarul de valori, poziția calificativului și cheia de catalog. Formalizatorul cere o potrivire pentru întreaga descriere și păstrează cuvintele originale. Aceeași declarație poate introduce o altă proprietate într-o altă lume. Declarațiile SOP · Verificările cititorului

O singură formalizare pentru pași separați

După o delimitare sigură, fiecare pas trece prin cititorul complet. O delimitare suplimentară poate fi verificată și când primul cuvânt are și sens de substantiv. Fiecare pas are nevoie de dovezi proprii pentru țintă, acțiune și argumente. Firele locale își păstrează cererea, iar referințele la predicatele instalate în lumi își păstrează identificatorii.

Când fiecare pas are o citire declarată completă, cititorul rezervă loc pentru combinațiile acelor citiri. Astfel, variantele lexicale parțiale ale fiecărui pas pot rămâne disponibile și combinația completă apare în aceeași listă de propuneri.

O expresie declarată în lumea limbii, cum ar fi „the new arrow”, poate desemna singurul obiect compatibil creat de pasul precedent. Formalizarea păstrează expresia și marchează ținta legată de acel pas. Identitatea concretă se stabilește în aplicație după crearea obiectului. Verificări pentru distanțe, repere și referințe

Distanța scrisă se leagă de acțiunea ei

O deplasare păstrează distanța și direcția, iar o plasare măsurată păstrează distanța și reperul. Lumea declară unitățile, predicatele și cheile admise. Numerele și unitățile fizice folosesc inventarele existente. Formalizatorul păstrează sensul lui „aproximativ” și lasă referințele la obiectele de pe pagină pentru etapa următoare. Contractul și exemplul

Un obiect și două repere

La o mutare „la jumătatea distanței dintre Deploy și Plan”, numai obiectul desemnat prin „it” este ținta mutării. Prolog separă cele două nume, iar un fir relațional le leagă de țintă și păstrează cerința de mijloc. Rezolvarea numelor și calculul poziției aparțin aplicației. Schema și comanda de reproducere

Câmpurile de date și expresiile care le leagă

Parserul de argumente rezervă o expresie întreagă, cum ar fi „and put the legend”. Articolul „the” poate astfel să facă parte dintr-un alt argument scris. Modulul pentru serii păstrează câmpul categoriei principale și câmpul care definește seriile. Aplicația rezolvă scopul scris „for the summary slide” și câmpurile din date. Contractul și exemplul de rulare

Leagă rolurile scrise înainte de alegerea interpretării

În „Immediately left of the shape, move them”, Prolog leagă destinația dinaintea virgulei de mutare și lasă obiectele desemnate de „them” de rezolvat în context. Un reper poate avea și o citire verbală dacă în poziția respectivă există dovezi pentru substantiv. Un modul opțional declară separat proprietățile „ține” și „poartă”. Categoriile de persoane și valorile admise provin din catalogul lumii. Contract și exemplu verificat

Ordinea cuvintelor și rolul sursei

O lume poate declara unde apare predicatul într-o construcție. Prolog leagă argumentele de pozițiile lor din text inclusiv când filtrul precedă comanda. Un cuantificator precum „all” poate desemna o categorie din inventarul lumii dacă numărul gramatical și operația sunt compatibile. Când textul numește documentul din care trebuie șters un paragraf, numai paragraful devine ținta ștergerii. Modulul relațional leagă documentul ca sursă. Fără el, cititorul păstrează fragmentul ca necitit. Exemple și limite observate

Read number pairs and respect list boundaries

The sentence “put house at 260,180” formerly left both coordinates unread. A world can declare a cue, action and two ordered numeric keys. The parser binds the two written values together, including signed decimals and parenthesized pairs. A third number or list of pairs prevents the pair reading. Quoted values stay literal. Clause splitting protects matched square-bracket lists containing commas, semicolons or imperative-looking items. Open-ended noun lists in scene descriptions can still lose objects and their attributes. World declarations · Paired benchmark

Keep domain choice downstream

The generator formerly scored target families from utterance-wide training associations. The twelve-candidate limit could then hide a less frequent SOP-declared meaning before discussion context was available. Training now retains action associations only. For a shared target phrase, search preserves a representative for each directly grounded action, span and declared family alongside the ordinary shortlist. Clause composition still has a finite search budget.

Literal payloads, names, spatial keys and shared preservation concepts have explicit SOP bindings. A domain can choose unrelated property names. The default CLI returns the proposed circuits. The separate pipeline can use an active tab to rank that set. Regression with fourteen new families · Meaning review on the held-out sample

First release

Commit 9596a53 introduced repo-local SWI-Prolog and Node tooling, a one-edit spelling checker and a learned English-to-SOP reader. It established a normalized-text holdout before measuring, so the exact same normalized utterance could never appear in both training and test. The first text-only run matched all semantic wires for 93 of 1,915 rows. Worker log · Measured baseline

Runtime knowledge and scoped grounding

Commit 73e150f added declarative runtime bindings, schema-aware substitution, request composition and copied-evidence checks. New world facts can supply vocabulary and typed constraints without retraining. Optional word pairs and protected terms improved spelling on the held-out injection benchmark. Independent contextual ranking and role attachment

The text formaliser and context estimator now run as separate stages. A schema tab preference can be weighed alongside compatible live entities and prior turns. The estimator reports the selected index, ranking and reasons while preserving valid alternatives. Lexical attachment uses supported action role markers and spatial relations from SOP facts. These cues help distinguish a direct target from an anchor and a selection constraint from a requested change. They do not supply an instance lookup result. Contextual estimator · Role attachment rules

A learned lexical association had supplied the sort roof for the pronoun it. Declared headless references now keep the thing unresolved while preserving the wording of a spatial relation and its anchor. Explicit spatial relation and anchor keys can license hypotheses for several families while the entity lookup remains unresolved. Role regressions

Structural validation and evidence checks leave semantic questions open. Pronoun resolution, source-to-target attachment and full preservation of modifiers are recurring problems in the independent review. Normalized-text holdouts measure variation within WebMeet. Template-grouped or independently collected evaluations would test broader generalisation. The experiment produces request circuits for subsequent interpretation and execution. WebMeet key validation

The first role-aware run identified 327 constraints that represented a spatial relation but omitted the application key. The SOP type checker accepted these wires. WebMeet requires an attribute on each constraint. The reader now grounds the relation and its landmark separately using the request key declarations and retains an unread phrase when the family or keys are unresolved. The landmark has its own lookup. Key resolution · Custom spatial bindings

Native frame evidence and literal accounting

Native English frame declarations now provide inspectable support for lexical fallback readings. A frame with distinct object and argument spans can now favor an in-scope target before Prolog selects its shortlist. Argument correspondences also expose literal evidence assigned to the wrong role. The quotation guard blocks predicates grounded only inside quoted commands. Generic single-argument frames supply no positive preference among already licensed readings of polysemous verbs. Frame matching and evidence ledger

Separate enumerated requests and same-target guards

Reading a noun list as one lexical target discarded later objects or attached their properties to the first. A clear list under one creation verb now offers a composite proposal with independently grounded objects and item-local modifiers. Explicitly coordinated actions also have a composition path. Uncertain list and landmark boundaries can still leave partial proposals. Composition rules

A conditional antecedent could resemble a separate action during clause splitting. The reader protects that boundary and can now encode a grounded scalar condition as a selector on the same existing target. The requested effect is represented separately. The mechanism relies on a declared condition cue, a same-target singular reference and an unambiguous world property. Scalar guard rules

Keep qualifiers, names and count questions attached to their targets

A cardinal or ordinal directly before a grounded noun can qualify the target. An adjective supported by independent training phrases can add a licensed property for that family. Explicit naming cues can attach a written name to a new target alongside separately grounded placement. The reader can also propose a count for “text boxes count?” when it grounds the whole noun phrase, and recognize a cue such as “every one of the” immediately before an existing target. Nominal qualifiers · Explicit names · Set readings

A declared count introduction can make an utterance interrogative even when it starts with a directive such as “give us a headcount of.” The reader binds the whole noun phrase. A written restriction such as “on the guest list” remains on the count as unresolved evidence when the list membership cannot be grounded. Prefix count controls

“All three arrows” describes a complete set and gives its cardinality. The target can retain both fill=all and count=3 with the written scope in count_words. A competing selector supported only by the quantifier’s words is removed from that reading. Counted universal regression

The same request body can reuse a training example when only declared edge politeness, interjections or terminal punctuation vary. The transfer preserves interrogative intent and checks that copied evidence still belongs to the body. The expressivity audit documents remaining catalog and binding gaps, including cross-property comparisons and reciprocal relations. Whole-body transfer · Expressivity audit · Measured grades

Learning with conflicting annotations

Requiring unanimity discarded the dominant reading of “in a row” when a few drawing annotations treated it as a direction. A normalized utterance now contributes at most one vote to each family and construction. Conflicting labels for one text count against acceptance. With disagreement, a reading needs at least 40 distinct supporting texts and 98% agreement. Post-nominal phrases also require two different noun sorts. The experiment retains original proposals and compares entire circuits. Support rule · Evidence and remaining limits

Keep each scene item in scope

Scene descriptions could collapse multiple objects into one target and apply the later objects’ colours or landmarks to it. A punctuated list of independently licensed objects can now produce separate requests with their own source spans. The reader checks for part and landmark attachments at possible boundaries. It also reapplies evidence-backed qualifiers inside each composed request, which can recover a missing size or count while preserving the other actions in that proposal. Ambiguous or unsupported attachments can stay unread. Scene-list regressions · Clause qualifier regressions

The orchard setting and afternoon can now form a request ahead of the five object clauses. Training must corroborate the local context request and its written values. A bare setting or an attested pattern for the same roles supports omission of the predicate. An unsupported setting stays available as unread evidence alongside the objects. Attach open text pairs to grounded nominals

A nominal phrase can introduce two text arguments, as with a chart of a measure grouped by a dimension. A world can declare the nominal cue, separator and pair of open span keys. The construction needs a grounded target and compatible action. It preserves the written values and keeps boundary ambiguity visible in the remaining evidence. Separate target identity from the requested effect

In “make the head small size,” the written head identifies an existing part and small is the requested result. Older examples encoded both fields as results. With a declared identity key, a unique selector before an independent result licenses a selector-phase alternative. Explicit assignment verbs, ambiguous spans and negated or conditional scopes suppress this construction. Identity and assignment regressions

The same locality requirement governs quantities and position phrases. A grounded result span can account for a noun repeated after the selected object. A declared nominal region or arrangement can take the place of a spatial pair that covers exactly the same words, while a second independently grounded landmark remains in the circuit. Bind native property and predicate meanings

When a compound noun also fixes a catalog class, the declaration can supply both the target and its result value. A native on/off frame can also resolve an explicit property name. The catalog can use the frame’s own value for a linked Boolean slot, or a world can declare an equivalent value such as show for grid lines. A trailing referent or an unbound qualifier such as “both” stays unread. Nominal class regressions · Fixed property regressions

A scale value may describe an object’s size or the degree of a change. Worlds can now license the object reading through a property’s declared scale and a local noun. The scale supplies canonical values while the constraint carries the submitted adjective. Scale regressions

Some target arguments mix a closed value such as a chart aggregate with written spans such as a measure and dimension. Ordered sequences let worlds declare these roles and separators together. The reader offers the sequence only when the binding is unique and agrees with the candidate’s existing fields. Argument-sequence regressions

Worlds can declare that a compact pair of written labels identifies an existing target. The pair is ordered by its declared span keys, and the noun and action identify its family. A competing pair or a payload outside the construction leaves the regular reader’s interpretations available. Compound reference regressions

Some property edits place the owner between the property phrase and the new value. The reader can carry the described owner and its typed property together when the native action and connectors identify both roles. Owner-bound edit regressions

Native request and question alternations can now propose a reading of an unchanged, contiguous body within the original utterance. The original wording stays attached and the reader requires a complete single request with locally grounded evidence. Alternation regressions

A known predicate alternation can support a training transfer when the complete argument evidence agrees with the source reading. Independent scene examples can also establish a boundary after a heading and before the object list. These proposals preserve the user’s wording and still pass the request catalog and evidence checks. Predicate regressions · Scene regressions

A fixed property that survives an isolated clause can fall out of the composed search. The reader can now restore that property in a proposal whose request has a uniquely located, disjoint scope. It retains existing requests and offers another circuit with the result attached. The contextual fallback checks for independently grounded values and external references before replacing a local reading. Scope and reference regressions

Property commands can reuse a lead and a grounded result across independent examples. The Prolog join requires the same family and action on each written segment, and a named owner needs its own support. Scene heading fragments and chained noun properties use separate local spans. Ground instance references and narrow actions

“Move block 12 right of block 10” can retain both full descriptions and their roles. “Align Process, Done, and n4” can retain all three written identifiers in one target when the code licenses its family. Shared code prefixes retain each compatible family. The text stage supplies these written selectors for downstream resolution. Instance reference regressions

When direct lookup misses a plural, the reader can use SOP inflection declarations to recover a licensed noun reading while keeping the written form. The learner also preserves supported verb modes at the predicate, act and family level. For a literal operation name, an omitted mode in a coarse annotation can coexist with independent evidence for that operation. Other verbs require consistent evidence for the act and mode within that family. Noun morphology regressions · Verb mode evidence · Measured grades

Bind coordinated roles and numeric qualifiers

A declared member action can bind several written labels to the same operation. The frame supplies the family, member key and phase, and the reader keeps the order and spelling of the labels, including a separator inside a quote. A new command or an unresolved member boundary can block the list reading. Member action regressions

A single edit can apply coordinated closed values to one independently grounded subject. The declared effect frame and catalog determine the property roles. Each value stays in the same result request, so “make the Crush box bold and italic” retains both effects on the Crush box. Coordinated effect regressions

An explicit numeric property can refine a named target or add a result. “Set n1 width 180 height ninety” attaches two results to n1, and an explicitly declared index can qualify a named removal. The reader checks the action, key and phase and leaves incompatible or competing numbers unresolved. Numeric qualifier regressions

Preserve requests before a closing courtesy sentence

A world can declare a closing sentence such as “That would help.” The reader offers a body reading when the closing sentence has its own boundary outside quotation. It keeps the original punctuation of the body and the full sequence of requests. The closing wording remains available as evidence. Closing discourse declarations · Trailing discourse regressions

Ground the operands of placement, creation and change

“Move mango to the left of pear” identifies a written target and a written landmark. A declared placement action and spatial marker can bound both labels even when they are lowercase. Family assignments remain alternatives and the landmark remains a lookup. Bare lowercase phrases with several words need independent evidence or quotation. A declared spatial surface such as “right next to” applies only where the action and relation are licensed. Named argument regressions

“Create an ellipse labelled Survey chart” uses the full written complement as the label. A declared creation role connects the named or quoted complement to a catalog key on a grounded new target. Individual clauses keep their own complements in a composed request. Unsupported tails can block an unquoted reading. Creation role regressions

“Widen n4” can use the comparative effect declared for the verb, and independent examples with distinct objects can establish a fixed effect such as the dashed style of a link. The result stays tied to its action and family. A conflict in a small set of examples blocks the learned effect. The shared learner permits up to 2% disagreement once at least 40 distinct witnesses support one meaning. Verb effect regressions

“Change the label on the house to Home Sweet Home” can now use the native drawing object inventory to ground the house. An exact link from the candidate property to an unrestricted string slot licenses the full label. Unknown nouns and conflicting inventory aliases leave the owner unresolved, and numeric or closed backing slots supply no open-text reading. Owner and backing-slot regressions

An ordered argument frame can declare the chart noun and its fixed type alongside its arguments. A scatter plot can bind a colour mapping or a regression-line effect and retain the plotted variables as unread. The catalog defines axis titles and has no keys for binding x and y data fields. A declared tail boundary preserves later qualifiers as unread when the frame lacks their roles. Chart declarations · Argument-sequence regressions

A unit can distinguish competing numeric property meanings. In “change the length of the design critique to an hour,” the installed duration field accepts hours and gives one compatible owner family. The result keeps the exact title and measure words. A known incompatible owner or two eligible measured properties prevent the inferred-owner reading. Measured owner regressions

“Move it slightly right” can now propose a direction without a landmark. A native motion frame licenses the direction for an existing owner and keeps an unresolved pronoun available for lookup. The drawing catalog distinguishes a qualitative distance from a numeric offset. The phrase “slightly” fills the qualitative distance key. A world that declares only a numeric offset retains the degree wording as unread. A number between a definite article and a typed singular noun stays in the written target description. In “shift the 11 block upwards a little,” the move has an upward direction and a qualitative distance, while 11 stays in the object description. The direction inventory includes vectors for recognizing a coordinated two-axis phrase. The current drawing move accepts one direction, so the full coordinated phrase stays unresolved. A written stopping clause remains attached to the movement it limits. Direction regressions

An indefinite noun phrase such as “a tiny lantern behind the pier” can now propose creation with its grounded size and landmark. The source contains no verb, so the creation request carries the original phrase. A finite clause, question, condition or distinct follow-up still needs its own reading. Nominal creation regressions

A transformation frame can ground a page-wide style or an object's material through the written owner and value. The catalog and native vocabulary license the property and value. For “coat the whole house in rough red brick,” the material is brick while “rough” remains unread. Transformation regressions · Held-out grades

Two regressions appeared when checking whole requests. A landmark such as “Rock 3” lost its numerical suffix. Written proper names now admit a final number. In “facing right next to the fence,” the longer spatial marker consumed the direction. The reader now also tries the shorter marker when the overlapping word completes a grounded property on the same target. Numbered names · Overlapping source words

Keep preservation and creation in their own scopes

“Keeping everything else where it is” used to blend into an earlier placement phrase. A native keep cue and a complete declared tail now offer a separate preservation request with a catalog-backed result. The written remainder or named selector stays available for instance lookup. Conditional tails, questions and incomplete scopes can still remain unread. Preservation adjunct regressions

A typed creation role can read a proper written title before or after its kind. A recognized kind with an explicit naming cue can also license a creation fragment without a verb. A role can require a native inventory match for a written operand such as a person’s given name. Creation role declarations

Keep additive quantities on new targets

“One more duck” formerly left the quantity unread even when the new duck was grounded. A native additive cue can now join a cardinal to the same local noun, and a declared “another” determiner can supply a single new item. The target retains the written quantity. Exact group aliases such as “a pair of” also qualify a grounded noun. Approximate amounts and explicit comparisons stay outside the exact count reading. Additive count regressions

Bound title substitutions when only a connector stays fixed

Two training examples, “Operations for Ops” and “Helpdesk for Support”, gave the reader a pattern that treated an entire instruction as a replacement label. When the fixed text consists only of grammar or punctuation and a slot has held only titles, training records its observed width. Prolog can accept a longer title with capitalized words and internal grammatical connectors. Slots anchored by a content word keep their existing behavior. A longer lowercase title can still be missed when the connector is the only evidence.

Keep a stated reason outside the next action

In “we do not need that part, drop group g1,” the first clause explains the instruction that follows. Native speaker pronouns, negation and desire vocabulary can identify that bounded statement before an independently grounded imperative. The reader preserves the statement as scoped unread evidence, with the command supplying the request predicate. A negated command, conditional scope or quoted imperative can still block the split. Preserve short commands with typed substitutions

In “distribute vertically”, the word left fixed by an aligned training example was the predicate itself. The old alignment required two fixed words and lost the axis. A short command can now reuse its unchanged predicate when each substituted argument resolves to a kind or a closed property of the same family. Two such arguments may stand next to one another. Open names and text payloads still need the ordinary alignment evidence.

Keep the words before and after spelling correction

Keep operands attached to their roles

Analytical frames declare which written words describe the new object, measured quantity, grouping and aggregation. They compile to the existing SWI-Prolog argument grammar. Bind a written topic to its own command

In “the huge bear: face right,” the noun and size describe an existing subject. The directional value belongs to the result.
